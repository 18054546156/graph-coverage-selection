# TDGS full audit and research decision record

Date: 2026-09-28

This document is the evidence ledger for the TDGS line. It supersedes numerical
claims in older planning notes when they conflict with the final Round 2b files.
It does not treat a plan, a queued Slurm job, or a positive expectation as an
experimental result.

## 1. Executive decision

The current evidence supports a narrow, reproducible statement:

> At a 2% per-class budget on the five selected MedMNIST tasks, adding a
> same-class endpoint coverage term to the global `A+A^2` Graph-A2 objective
> improves paired mean balanced accuracy over the regenerated Graph-A2
> baseline by +1.57 percentage points at five training seeds. The improvement
> is +0.71 pp after excluding TissueMNIST, where the effect is largest. At 5%,
> the pooled gain is only +0.39 pp, with 2/5 datasets improving, so the claim
> is budget-specific.

The evidence does **not** support the stronger statements that:

- inverse-frequency demand weighting is an independently useful contribution;
- the exact within-class geometry is the source of the gain on all datasets;
- the method is universally effective across selection budgets;
- the method is validated for patient-level or clinical deployment;
- the contribution is a new class-aware facility-location principle.

For the 2% benchmark, the recommended present algorithm is `tdgs_mask` or,
if the original naming is retained, `tdgs_cls` with the class-flat weight
demoted to an implementation detail; the mask-only 5% control is not yet run.
The best next research route is a gated
boundary-aware extension, but it is a **proposal**, not a current result.

### 2.2 Protocol actually used

- PathMNIST, BloodMNIST, OrganAMNIST, OrganSMNIST, TissueMNIST.
- Frozen UNI 224x224 embeddings for selection.
- Global kNN, `k=50`, symmetric degree normalization, `K=A_sym+A_sym^2`.
- ResNet-18 from scratch, 1000 epochs, batch size 256, final checkpoint.
- Balanced accuracy (BA) is the primary endpoint.
- Equal per-class quota, with `bpc=int(N*ratio)//C`.
- Ratios 2% and 5%.
- New-arm training seeds are 42--46; selection seed is fixed at 42.
- No test or target data is used to construct the selector or tune its parameters.

The released repository's CLI default (`k=10`, per-class graph scope) differs
from the paper/archive protocol (`k=50`, global scope). The project correctly
replayed and used the latter. This distinction must be stated in the paper.

The selection-space checks were useful: `J(graph_a2,tdgs_cls)` ranged from

## 4. Code-level interpretation

The actual `greedy_blended` objective is

`F(S) = (1-lambda) sum_i w1_i max_j K_ij + lambda sum_i w2_i max_j A_ij`,

under an equal per-class partition constraint. For `tdgs_cls` at lambda 0.5,
`A = M * K / max(K)`, where `M_ij=1[y_i=y_j]`, `w1=1`, and
`w2=1/(C*n_class(i))`. Thus the method is not simply a pure masked objective:
it retains a global coverage term. `tdgs_mask` keeps the same blend and mask but
uses uniform task weights; `tdgs_wcls` keeps global credit and changes weights.

The code also makes two important theoretical facts testable:

1. The no-mask uniform factorial cell is a positive rescaling of Graph-A2, so
   its selected set should be identical. The runtime identity check exists.
2. At lambda=1, after masking and with every class quota filled, the class-flat
   row weight is constant within each class. It cannot change the within-class
   greedy ordering. The `--assert-class-weight-identity` check exists, but a
   five-dataset formal HPC run should still be archived before using it as a
   paper result.

Both statements concern selected IDs and optimization structure, not accuracy.

The constraint is a partition matroid, not an ordinary cardinality constraint.
The generic greedy guarantee is therefore the appropriate partition-matroid
guarantee (1/2 in the standard monotone-submodular setting). At lambda=1 the
objective decomposes by class, so independent per-class greedy has the usual
cardinality-style (1-1/e) guarantee for each block. This is a mathematical
property of the endpoint, not an empirical superiority claim.

## 5. Facts, unsupported hypotheses, and post-hoc explanations

### Verified facts

- The final 2% main contrast is positive in all five dataset means at five
  training seeds.
- `tdgs_mask` is close to `tdgs_cls` and weight-only is not independently
  positive.
- The 5% main contrast does not meet the predeclared 4/5 same-sign gate.
- The probe-based difficulty and direction additions did not help.
- The global and per-class graph operators select different sets.
- The selection-space change tracks the corrected cross-class share in the five
  datasets, but this is a five-point descriptive relation.
- The data have no patient/site metadata sufficient for patient-level
  validation; MedMNIST arrays cannot supply that information retroactively.

### Not supported

- “Balanced accuracy is optimized” is not proved; class-flat demand weighting
  only mirrors the macro aggregation in the metric.
- “The two-hop cross-class path is causal” is not proved; the planned
  mask-before-propagation control has code but no final five-dataset training
  result.
- “The exact within-class geometry is necessary” is not supported after the
  non-Tissue permutation contrast becomes small.
- “The method generalizes to clinical data” is not tested.
- “The method is superior at every budget” is contradicted by the 5% result.
- Pooled standard errors over 25 cells do not create 25 independent datasets;
  OrganA and OrganS share source volumes and the effective N is about 2--3.

### Post-hoc explanations, to label as such

The claim that 5% enters a flat region because Graph-A2 already covers the
dominant modes is plausible and consistent with the observed budget pattern,
but it was not preregistered as a causal test. The explanation that Tissue is
helped because its high cross-class K share leaves more cross-boundary credit to
remove is also a mechanism hypothesis; the dataset-level Spearman is based on
five points and is not a general law.

## 6. Novelty audit from original papers

The following source pages were read directly on 2026-09-28:

1. Wei, Iyer, Bilmes, ICML 2015, PMLR 37,
   `https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/wei15.html`. Its NN/classwise coverage
   formulation already establishes class-conditioned submodular selection.
2. Rustamov et al., arXiv:2606.22002v1,
   `https://arxiv.org/html/2606.22002`. It defines global kNN, `A_sym+A_sym^2`,
   per-class budgets, and discusses global versus per-class construction.
3. Mirzasoleiman et al., CRAIG, arXiv:1906.01827,
   `https://arxiv.org/html/1906.01827`. It supplies a class-aware coreset line,
   although its core object is gradient approximation rather than graph
   coverage.
4. Killamsetty et al., GLISTER, AAAI 2021,
   `https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16988`, is a
   validation-set/bilevel selection line; use this published page rather than
   the withdrawn arXiv HTML endpoint.
5. Kothawade et al., SIMILAR, NeurIPS 2021,
   `https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00717`, is a target/query-aware submodular
   information line; it is not the source-only setting here.
6. Cui et al., Class-Balanced Loss, arXiv:1901.05555,
   `https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05555`, already motivates inverse-frequency-like
   class reweighting, although it applies the weights to training loss rather
   than a selection demand measure.

Novelty verdict:

- Not novel alone: class-aware coverage, inverse-frequency weighting, global
  graph construction, multi-hop propagation, per-class quotas, facility
  location, and greedy selection.
- Plausibly novel as a **specific mechanism study**: retain the global graph's
  propagation geometry, add a same-class endpoint credit term, and test it
  against global credit and per-class graph reconstruction under a fixed quota.
- Stronger medical novelty is still absent. A high-level medical imaging paper
  needs either a real acquisition/site/patient problem or a better validated
  boundary/shift objective, not only a new name for a masked facility-location
  variant.

## 7. Three research routes

### Route A: Class-compatible coverage (current baseline route)

`R_ij=M_ij K_ij`, maximize `sum_i max_{j in S} R_ij` with fixed per-class
quota, optionally blended with global coverage. This is the lowest-risk route:
it has the current evidence, transparent controls and low selection cost. Its
novelty ceiling is moderate, so it requires stronger baselines, another
encoder/backbone and a real dataset to target a high-level journal.

### Route B: Boundary-aware class-compatible coverage (recommended research
route, not yet validated)

Let

`q_i = sum_j K_ij 1[y_j != y_i] / (sum_j K_ij + eps)`.

Within each class, convert `q_i` to a rank weight `r_i` with mean one and define

`mu_i = (1/(C*n_c)) r_i`.

Use

`F_B(S) = (1-lambda) F_G(S)/F_G(P)
          + lambda sum_i mu_i max_{j in S} M_ij*Kbar_ij / F_B(P)`.

This keeps each class's total prior mass unchanged and uses only source labels
and the frozen graph. It specifically tests whether boundary demands, rather
than all samples equally, are the medically useful coverage failures. Its
additional cost is one sparse row reduction and within-class ranking,
`O(nnz(K)+N log N)` CPU; greedy cost is the same order as the current masked
selector. It remains a nonnegative weighted max-coverage objective, so
monotone submodularity is preserved.

This route must be compared only against Graph-A2, `tdgs_mask`,
`a2_perclass`, and a boundary-rank permutation/null. Do not tune rank caps or
lambda on the test set. First screen at 2%, three seeds, then confirm one
predeclared candidate at both budgets and five seeds.

### Route C: Patient/site-robust coverage

If a real dataset provides patient, site, scanner, stain or acquisition group,
define groups `g` and add a group-conditional term, for example

`F_R(S) = F_B(S) + eta sum_g pi_g sum_i in g max_{j in S, y_j=y_i} R_ij`.

Use patient-level splits and report BA as primary, with site-stratified BA and
worst-group recall secondary. This has the highest medical relevance but cannot
be validated on the present MedMNIST arrays. It is a separate data acquisition
requirement, not an analysis that can be inferred from current results.

## 8. Preferred route: boundary-aware CCB-FL

### 8.1 Why it is the next falsifiable step

Route A shows a problem with credit scope but only modest novelty. Route B turns
the observed moderator into an explicit, source-only selection principle:
allocate within-class coverage attention to points whose global neighborhood is
most class-mixed. The prediction is made before downstream training. If it does
not beat `tdgs_mask`, the route stops; it is not rescued with post-hoc tuning.

### 8.2 Algorithm

1. Normalize frozen embeddings and build the protocol graph with `k=50`.
2. Build `K=A_sym+A_sym^2`.
3. Compute each point's global class-mixing score `q_i` from K.
4. Rank q within each class. Use a fixed rank transform with no learned
   parameter, normalize its class mean to one, and multiply by `1/(C*n_c)`.
5. Build `R=M*K/max(K)` and optimize the lambda=0.5 blend of global and
   masked coverage under the existing partition quota.
6. Save selected IDs, q/rank diagnostics, kernel checksum, config, seed and
   code commit with every run.

The `tdgs_boundary_select.py` module added with this audit contains the
source-only score construction and reuses the existing greedy implementation;
it is a research candidate, not a completed downstream result.

### 8.3 Compute and memory

The boundary score is computed by sparse row reductions over K. It adds no
GPU training and does not change the sparse greedy asymptotic cost. The dominant
cost remains constructing/storing `A_sym^2` and maintaining coverage vectors.
For a kNN graph with N nodes and k neighbors, the sparse kernel is roughly
`O(N k^2)` worst-case before pruning; the boundary statistic itself is
`O(nnz(K))`. The implementation must report actual nnz and wall time rather
than relying only on this bound.

### 8.4 Decision gates

Continue to confirmation only if, in a three-seed 2% screen, boundary-aware is
positive versus `tdgs_mask` on at least 4/5 datasets, pooled paired BA is at
least +0.5 pp, and worst-class recall is not lower by more than 0.5 pp pooled.
Stop Route B if the selected set is nearly identical to `tdgs_mask` and BA is
not different, if the gain occurs only in Tissue, or if pooled BA is below
+0.5 pp. Pivot to Route C if a real site/patient dataset is available; otherwise
publish Route A as a mechanism/negative-budget study with restrained claims.

The confirmation run is fixed before looking at its results: both ratios,
seeds 42--46, five datasets, BA primary, paired seed contrasts, per-dataset
means, exclusion of Tissue as a sensitivity analysis, and a predeclared
boundary-rank permutation null. No test-set tuning, lambda sweep, cap sweep, or
metric expansion is allowed.

## 9. Minimal verification plan

### Phase 0: close the audit

- Freeze one Table 1 harvest and manifest; resolve the 394/400 versus 400/400
  denominator discrepancy.
- Run the existing identity checks and archive their JSON, including
  `lambda=1` class-weight Jaccard.
- Mark `mask_before_prop` as selection-space only until a downstream result is
  actually run.
- Update all old 145-cell/“running” text or mark it historical.

### Phase 1: current method, strong comparison

- Main table: Graph-A2, `tdgs_mask`, selected winner, and all author baselines.
- Controls: `a2_perclass`, `tdgs_cls_perm`, `tdgs_wcls` at 2%; do not claim a
  complete 5% factorial because it is not present.
- Report paired seed deltas, mean rank, dataset-level signs and effective N.
- Use worst-class recall only as a declared secondary endpoint.

### Phase 2: boundary-aware screen

- Run only Graph-A2, `tdgs_mask`, and boundary-aware CCB-FL at 2%, seeds 42--44.
- Add the boundary-rank permutation null at the same seed.
- Use the gates in Section 8.4; do not add other variants after seeing outcomes.

### Phase 3: confirmation and representation robustness

- Confirm the single surviving candidate at 2% and 5%, seeds 42--46.
- Repeat selection with one independent frozen medical encoder or a fixed
  alternative representation. The encoder choice must be fixed before reading
  the downstream scores.
- Repeat downstream training with one additional fixed backbone only after the
  selection-space result is understood.

### Phase 4: independent medical validation

- Use one real dataset with patient-level split and site/scanner/stain metadata.
- Keep BA primary and report site-stratified BA, worst-group recall and a
  confidence interval as secondary analyses.
- Test whether the gain survives a site-held-out evaluation. If not, the method
  is a benchmark mechanism study, not a robust medical selection method.

## 10. Paper plan

1. A reproducible diagnosis that global graph coverage can grant credit across
   class endpoints despite a fixed class quota.
2. A class-compatible endpoint mask that yields a +1.57 pp 2% BA gain over the
   regenerated Graph-A2 baseline at five seeds, with an explicit 5% limitation.

   not reliable under frozen-UNI/ResNet-18 mismatch.

## 11. Final evidence boundary

The project is ready to write a careful mechanism paper around the 2% result,
but not yet ready to claim a high-level, clinically general data-selection
framework. The minimum upgrade for that claim is not another name or another
metric: it is a preregistered boundary-aware test, an independent representation
test, and a patient/site-held-out medical dataset. Until those exist, the paper
should say exactly what was learned: **coverage credit scope matters under a
low-budget, fixed-quota protocol; class-flat weighting and proxy demand are not
the validated novelty.**
